# MEDI-CAPS UNIVERSITY, INDORE
## ACADEMIC REGULATIONS FOR UNDERGRADUATE AND POSTGRADUATE PROGRAMMES (2024-2025)
**Document Reference:** MCU/ACAD/REG/2024/01  
**Applicability:** All Faculty of Engineering, Faculty of Management, Faculty of Science, and Faculty of Pharmacy  
**Effective Date:** July 1, 2024  

---

### SECTION 1: PREAMBLE AND DEFINITIONS

#### § 1.1 Statutory Authority and Scope
1. These regulations shall be designated as the Medi-Caps University Academic Regulations (2024-2025) and shall govern the admission, curriculum framework, continuous evaluation, semester examinations, credit requirements, grading mechanisms, and award of undergraduate (B.Tech, BCA, B.Sc, BBA, B.Com, B.Pharm) and postgraduate (M.Tech, MCA, MBA, M.Sc) degrees.
2. The regulations are enacted under Section 24 of the Medi-Caps University Act (Madhya Pradesh Niji Vishwavidyalaya Adhiniyam) and approved by the Academic Council and Governing Body of Medi-Caps University, Indore.
3. Every student enrolled in the university shall be subject to the provisions contained herein. Ignorance of any clause or sub-clause shall not constitute an admissible defense against penalties or regulatory decisions.

#### § 1.2 Key Terminology
1. **"University"** signifies Medi-Caps University, AB Road, Pigdambar, Rau, Indore, Madhya Pradesh - 453331.
2. **"Academic Council"** refers to the apex academic governing authority of the University responsible for curriculum, academic quality, and regulatory maintenance.
3. **"Dean of Academic Affairs"** denotes the university official administering university-wide academic curricula, examination eligibility, and instructional delivery.
4. **"Head of Department (HoD)"** refers to the administrative and academic executive of an individual university teaching department.
5. **"Choice Based Credit System (CBCS)"** defines the modular instructional paradigm enabling students to select core courses, professional electives, open electives, and non-credit audit courses.
6. **"Continuous Internal Assessment (CIA)"** represents the progressive formative evaluation comprising mid-semester tests, quizzes, assignments, case presentations, and laboratory practical assessments.
7. **"End-Semester Examination (ESE)"** denotes the summative formal examination conducted by the Controller of Examinations at the conclusion of each academic semester.

### SECTION 2: CURRICULAR STRUCTURE AND CREDIT SYSTEM

#### § 2.1 Credit Weightage Formulation
1. Academic instruction is quantified in terms of credits. One semester credit represents:
   - **Lecture (L):** One hour of classroom lecture per week per semester equals 1.0 credit.
   - **Tutorial (T):** One hour of interactive problem-solving tutorial per week per semester equals 1.0 credit.
   - **Practical / Laboratory (P):** Two hours of laboratory or workshop practical session per week per semester equals 1.0 credit.
2. A standard four-year Bachelor of Technology (B.Tech) degree requires the satisfactory completion of 160 earned credits.
3. A two-year Master of Business Administration (MBA) or Master of Computer Applications (MCA) requires the satisfactory completion of 88 and 80 earned credits respectively.
4. An undergraduate student cannot register for more than 26 credits in any regular semester, nor less than 16 credits, except in the final graduating semester with prior approval from the Dean of Academic Affairs.

#### § 2.2 Course Categorization and Electives
1. The curriculum comprises Humanities & Social Sciences (HSS), Basic Sciences (BSC), Engineering Sciences (ESC), Professional Core Courses (PCC), Professional Electives (PEC), Open Electives (OEC), and Mandatory Non-Credit Courses (MNC).
2. Students must register for open elective courses from departments other than their parent discipline to promote interdisciplinary scholarship.
3. Honors Degree Option: Undergraduate B.Tech students who maintain a cumulative grade point average (CGPA) of 7.50 or higher up to the end of the fourth semester may register for an additional 20 credits through designated advanced tracks or approved SWAYAM/NPTEL MOOC certifications to graduate with B.Tech (Honors).

### SECTION 3: COURSE REGISTRATION AND SEMESTER ENROLLMENT

#### § 3.1 Pre-Registration and Regular Registration
1. Every candidate must complete online course registration on the Medi-Caps ERP portal within five working days before the start of each semester.
2. Registration is conditional upon full clearance of university tuition fees, library dues, laboratory clearances, and hostel liabilities.
3. Late course registration is permitted up to seven calendar days after classes commence, subject to a late registration administrative fee of Rs. 1,000. Registration beyond seven days is strictly disallowed under any circumstances.

#### § 3.2 Add/Drop of Elective Courses
1. A student may drop or swap an elective course within ten working days of the semester commencement upon written endorsement by the Departmental Faculty Advisor and approval of the HoD.
2. Minimum class strength for offering any professional or open elective is fifteen (15) students. If enrollment is less than fifteen, the course stands cancelled and enrolled students must select an alternative elective.

### SECTION 4: ATTENDANCE REGULATIONS AND EXAM ELIGIBILITY
*(PLANTED CONTRADICTION CLAUSE 4.2)*

#### § 4.1 Attendance Calculation Framework
1. Attendance shall be monitored on an hourly basis for all scheduled lectures, tutorials, and practical laboratories via the automated campus biometric or RFID portal.
2. The percentage of attendance shall be calculated as:  
   $$\text{Attendance Percentage} = \left( \frac{\text{Total Contact Hours Attended}}{\text{Total Contact Hours Delivered}} \right) \times 100$$
3. Faculty members must upload attendance records to the ERP weekly, and consolidated attendance sheets must be displayed on departmental notice boards before each Mid-Semester Test.

#### § 4.2 Mandatory Minimum Attendance Requirement (PLANTED CONTRADICTION 1)
1. **Strict 75% Attendance Rule:** Every registered student must achieve a minimum attendance of 75% in theory lectures and practical/tutorial classes separately in each registered course to be eligible to appear for the End-Semester Examination (ESE).
2. **Absolute Non-Condonation Clause:** The Head of Department, Dean of Faculty, and Dean of Academic Affairs possess NO authority to condone attendance below 75% under any standard administrative procedure, personal hardship, or monetary penalty.
3. **Medical Board Exemption Exception:** Condonation below 75% may ONLY be granted on severe medical grounds requiring prolonged hospitalization, duly certified by a Medical Superintendent of a recognized tertiary-care hospital and validated by the official Medi-Caps University Medical Board. Even with Medical Board certification, an absolute minimum of 70.0% attendance is legally non-negotiable. If attendance drops below 70.0%, the student is unconditionally debarred from the End-Semester Examination.
4. **Debarment and Backlog Status:** Any student falling below 75% (or 70% with Medical Board clearance) in a subject is declared "Debarred due to Short Attendance" (Grade 'DE') and must re-register for the course in full during the subsequent academic year or summer term. No condonation fee, special fine, or executive waiver is admissible.

#### § 4.3 Duty Leave and Institutional Representation
1. Students selected to represent the University in state, national, or international academic conventions, cultural festivals, or approved athletic meets may be granted Duty Leave (DL) for the verified days of participation, up to a maximum of 10% of total contact hours.
2. Requisite approvals must be submitted in advance to the Head of Department along with the official nomination memo from the Dean of Student Welfare.

### SECTION 5: ASSESSMENT METHODOLOGY AND EXAMINATION SCHEME

#### § 5.1 Continuous Internal Assessment (CIA) - 40 Marks
1. For theory courses with a 100-mark total scheme, the weightage distribution is:
   - **Continuous Internal Assessment (CIA):** 40 Marks
   - **End-Semester Examination (ESE):** 60 Marks
2. The 40 marks of Continuous Internal Assessment are structured as follows:
   - Mid-Semester Test 1 (MST-1): 15 Marks (covers first 40% syllabus).
   - Mid-Semester Test 2 (MST-2): 15 Marks (covers remaining 60% syllabus).
   - Continuous Evaluation / Quizzes / Faculty Assignments: 10 Marks.
3. A student who misses an MST due to genuine medical emergency must submit a medical certificate to the HoD within three days of resuming campus. If approved, a single Comprehensive Makeup Test covering 100% syllabus shall be administered before semester end.

#### § 5.2 End-Semester Examination (ESE) - 60 Marks
1. The End-Semester Examination is conducted centrally by the Office of the Controller of Examinations.
2. The examination duration is three (3) hours for a standard 60-mark paper, consisting of both analytical and theoretical problem-solving questions.
3. Admit cards (Hall Tickets) are generated through the ERP only for students who have cleared all university fees and met the statutory attendance threshold.

### SECTION 6: PASSING CRITERIA, GRADING SCALE AND PROHIBITION OF GRACE MARKS
*(PLANTED CONTRADICTION CLAUSE 6.3)*

#### § 6.1 Ten-Point Grading System
The University operates an absolute 10-point letter grading system mapped to percentage scores as defined below:

| Percentage Range | Letter Grade | Grade Point | Performance Classification |
|:-----------------|:------------:|:-----------:|:---------------------------|
| 90% and above    | O            | 10.0        | Outstanding                |
| 80% to 89.9%     | A+           | 9.0         | Excellent                  |
| 70% to 79.9%     | A            | 8.0         | Very Good                  |
| 60% to 69.9%     | B+           | 7.0         | Good                       |
| 50% to 59.9%     | B            | 6.0         | Above Average              |
| 45% to 49.9%     | C            | 5.0         | Average                    |
| 40% to 44.9%     | P            | 4.0         | Pass                       |
| Below 40%        | F            | 0.0         | Fail                       |
| Absent           | Ab           | 0.0         | Absent                     |
| Debarred         | DE           | 0.0         | Debarred (Short Attendance)|

#### § 6.2 Calculation of SGPA and CGPA
1. **Semester Grade Point Average (SGPA)**:
   $$\text{SGPA} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^{n} (C_i \times G_i)}{\sum_{i=1}^{n} C_i}$$
   where $C_i$ denotes the credits assigned to course $i$ and $G_i$ represents the grade points earned in course $i$.
2. **Cumulative Grade Point Average (CGPA)**:
   $$\text{CGPA} = \frac{\sum_{j=1}^{m} (C_j \times G_j)}{\sum_{j=1}^{m} C_j}$$
   computed over all courses registered across all completed semesters.

#### § 6.3 Passing Standards and Absolute Prohibition of Grace Marks (PLANTED CONTRADICTION 2)
1. **Two-Tier Passing Threshold:** To pass any credit-bearing theory course, a student must secure:
   - A minimum of 40% marks (i.e., 24 out of 60 marks) in the End-Semester Examination; AND
   - A minimum of 40% marks (i.e., 40 out of 100 marks) in aggregate combining Continuous Internal Assessment (CIA) and the End-Semester Examination.
2. For laboratory courses, the minimum passing mark is 50% in continuous practical evaluation and 50% in the practical end-semester viva-voce examination.
3. **Strict Prohibition of Grace Marks:** In strict adherence to UGC autonomous guidelines and Academic Council Resolution AC/2023/IV-12, **no grace marks, moderation adjustments, or compensatory scoring shall be awarded under any circumstances**.
4. **No Discretionary Moderation:** Neither the Controller of Examinations, Dean of Academic Affairs, nor the Vice-Chancellor possesses statutory authority to award grace marks. If a student secures 39 out of 100 in aggregate or 23 out of 60 in the End-Semester Examination, the student is awarded an 'F' grade and must re-appear in the backlog examination. There is zero tolerance for round-ups or discretionary bonus marks.

### SECTION 7: PROMOTION CRITERIA AND YEAR DETENTION (YEAR-BACK)

#### § 7.1 Credit Thresholds for Promotion
1. **From 1st Year to 2nd Year (3rd Semester):** A student must earn a minimum of 50% of the total registered credits in the 1st and 2nd semesters combined.
2. **From 2nd Year to 3rd Year (5th Semester):** A student must clear all credits of the 1st year (zero backlogs in Semester 1 and 2) AND earn at least 50% credits of the 2nd year.
3. **From 3rd Year to 4th Year (7th Semester):** A student must clear all credits of the 1st and 2nd years (zero backlogs in Semesters 1, 2, 3, and 4) AND earn at least 50% credits of the 3rd year.
4. Any student who fails to satisfy the promotional threshold is placed on **"Year Detention" (Year-Back)** and must spend the following academic year clearing their pending backlog examinations before progressing to the subsequent academic level.

#### § 7.2 Maximum Duration for Degree Completion
1. For four-year degree programmes (e.g., B.Tech, B.Pharm), the maximum permissible duration to satisfy all graduation requirements is six (6) academic years ($N + 2$ years rule).
2. For three-year undergraduate programmes (e.g., BCA, B.Sc, BBA), the maximum duration is five (5) academic years.
3. Failure to complete all academic requirements within the stipulated maximum period shall result in automatic cancellation of university registration and de-enrollment.

### SECTION 8: BRANCH CHANGE POLICY (B.TECH FIRST YEAR)

#### § 8.1 Eligibility and Modalities
1. Branch change is considered strictly at the conclusion of the First Year (Second Semester) and applies only against vacancies existing within the sanctioned intake of the receiving discipline.
2. **Eligibility Criteria:**
   - The candidate must have cleared all courses in both Semester 1 and Semester 2 in their very first attempt without any backlog or make-up examination.
   - The candidate must possess a minimum CGPA of 8.00 at the end of the second semester.
   - The candidate must not have faced any disciplinary proceedings or anti-ragging complaints.
3. The total number of students permitted to migrate into a discipline shall not exceed 10% of the sanctioned intake of that branch.
4. The branch change allocation is determined strictly in order of merit based on 1st-year CGPA. In case of a tie, marks obtained in 10+2 Mathematics and Physics shall act as the tie-breaker. Once accepted, a change of branch is irrevocable.

### SECTION 9: RE-EVALUATION, RETOTALING, AND SCRIPT INSPECTION

#### § 9.1 Verification and Retotaling
1. A candidate may apply for verification and retotaling of their answer script within seven (7) working days of the publication of semester results through the ERP portal on payment of Rs. 300 per course.
2. Retotaling encompasses verifying whether all answers have been evaluated and that marks have been accurately transcribed to the marks card.

#### § 9.2 Challenge Re-Evaluation
1. If dissatisfied with the result following retotaling, a candidate may apply for Challenge Re-Evaluation within ten (10) days of retotaling results upon payment of Rs. 1,500 per course.
2. The Controller of Examinations shall redact candidate identity markers and assign the answer script to two independent external examiners.
3. If the average score awarded by the two external examiners deviates by more than 15% from the original score, the new score replaces the initial mark.
4. If the revised score shows an increase of 20% or more, 50% of the challenge fee (Rs. 750) shall be refunded to the student.

### SECTION 10: ACADEMIC HONORS AND DEGREE CLASSIFICATION

#### § 10.1 First Class with Distinction and Dean's List
1. A candidate who secures a final CGPA of 8.50 and above, without having failed in any course throughout the entire duration of the programme, shall be declared to have passed in **"First Class with Distinction"**.
2. **Dean’s Merit List:** Students securing an SGPA of 9.00 and above in any semester with zero backlog courses are placed on the "Dean’s Academic Merit Honor Roll" and awarded a formal commendation certificate at the Annual Convocation ceremony.
3. A student who has appeared in any supplementary, backlog, or improvement examination is automatically ineligible for the award of First Class with Distinction or University Rank Medals.

#### § 10.2 Award of Medals
1. **Chancellor's Gold Medal:** Awarded to the graduating student with the highest overall CGPA across all undergraduate disciplines of the University.
2. **Vice-Chancellor's Silver Medal:** Awarded to the topper in each individual degree programme, provided the graduate has completed the degree in the minimum prescribed duration without any disciplinary reprimand.
